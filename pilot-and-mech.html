Two connected characters

Your PILOT is the person: their background, skills, relationships, and reasons for being in the cockpit. Your MECH is the machine they bring to missions: a frame with weapons, systems, and a job it performs for the squad.

A pilot can change mechs, and a mech can be refitted. Treat your first choices as a direction to explore, not a permanent identity. The pilot side helps shape who your character is during narrative scenes, while the mech side determines many of the options available during tactical combat.

These two sides do not have to describe the exact same personality. A cautious pilot might operate an aggressive mech, or a bold pilot might prefer a machine built around protecting the squad. That contrast can become an interesting part of the character.

Build Tips

  1. Start with a general idea of what you want your pilot and mech to do: protect allies, control space, move quickly, deal damage, or support the team.
  2. Choose weapons and systems that work toward that role instead of selecting each option independently.
  3. Check your mech's range, movement, defenses, and heat before finalizing your equipment. A strong build should be able to function at the distance and pace you prefer.
  4. Look for combinations between your frame, weapons, systems, talents, and licenses. Your choices should support one another rather than compete for the same actions or resources.
  5. Make sure you have at least one reliable backup option prepared that you can spend your action economy on in case you're forced into a position where your main gameplan is shut off.
  6. Use COMP/CON to compare choices, but select equipment because you understand and enjoy it rather than simply copying an allegedly optimal build.

A "good" build is one you understand well enough to make decisions with. A simple build that you enjoy piloting, with a toolkit that you know how to operate well is a better first step than a complicated, theoretically "better" build copied from someone else that you don't know how to play.

Pilot-side Gameplay

The pilot portion of gameplay that takes plase outside of your mech is largely focused on narrative, and rather rules‐light.

Pilot skills, talents, triggers, and backgrounds help answer what your character knows and how they respond to problems outside of combat. They can describe formal training, a past career, a personal obsession, or the habits your pilot developed while surviving in the Diaspora.

When choosing these options, think about what kinds of scenes you want to contribute to. A pilot who is good at reading people will create different opportunities from one who specializes in machines, research, wilderness travel, or direct action.

Mech-side Gameplay

Your frame gives the mech its basic strengths, while weapons and systems determine how those strengths appear during a mission. Consider your preferred range, how much movement you need, and whether you want to spend turns attacking, defending, controlling enemies, or helping allies.

It is also useful to notice what your build does not do. A mech that excels at close combat may need a plan for distant targets, while a fragile artillery build may depend on allies creating space for it to work safely.

Expect your build to change

LANCER gives you opportunities to improve and refit your character as the campaign continues. New licenses can add equipment that changes how your mech operates, while core bonuses and pilot advancement can strengthen the parts of the build you enjoy most.

Do not treat an early choice as a mistake just because your priorities change. Trying a different weapon, frame, or role is part of learning what kind of lancer you want to become.

Pick a question, not a perfect answer

Try questions such as “Do I want to protect allies?”, “Do I want to control space?”, or “Do I want to get close and hit hard?” Your answer gives you a starting role without asking you to know every manufacturer or frame. Once you have a direction, choose a few options that reinforce it and leave room to learn what you enjoy during play.
