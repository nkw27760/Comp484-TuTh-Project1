A long time ahead in a galaxy not-so-far away...

LANCER takes place in a far-future where humanity has spread across a myriad different worlds in the Milky Way. Advanced technology connects distant communities, but it does not make every place equal or peaceful.

The galaxy is mainly made up of two types of clusters (groups of starsystems). The Core Worlds, where the vast majority of humanity resides and Union has a strong foothold, and the Diaspora, a massive expanse of frontier colonies, independent communities, and people trying to build something better after old systems failed.

A campaign might focus on Union-aligned nations, or people living beyond its reach. But typically there's a mix of both, characters who are caught between several powers all trying to amass more influence and meet their own ends. The lore and universe are left deliberately vast and open-ended so that technically, any campaign could still reasonably take place within the Orion Arm. That gives the setting room for anything; grounded military missions, fantastical open exploration, political intrigue, even paranormal cosmic horror.

What is Union?

Union is the largest political faction in the setting: a government that claims to protect human life and create a more just future. In the Core Worlds, Union provides security, infrastructure, education, and access to advanced technology. Its influence becomes less direct in the Diaspora, where colonies and local governments have more independence.

Union is powerful, but it is not perfect or all-controlling. Its ideals often conflict with old systems, corporate interests, local traditions, and the realities of governing an entire galaxy. Some people see Union as a hopeful force working toward a better future, while others view it as distant, bureaucratic, or even hypocritical.

The Major Manufacturers

Most mechs and their equipment come from one of five major manufacturers. Each has a distinct reputation and design philosophy.

Oftentimes, a Lancer will have prior experience with at least one of these companies. Though it isn't a strict requirement, it can help to inform the character's backstory and personal motivations.

GMS

General Massive Systems makes dependable, widely available equipment. They are known as the "galactic standard," and for good reason, their Everest chassis is as ubiquitous in civilian sectors as it is on the battlefield, and it's the main backbone of Union's military; every Lancer has piloted at least one GMS-line chassis. GMS is a practical starting point for pilots who want reliable tools without a narrow specialty.

IPS-N

Interplanetary Shipping-Northstar initially started out as a freighting and logistics company, and thus needed equipment that could endure long hauls, hard labor, and dangerous frontier routes. Its modern offerings follow the same design principle. With an emphasis on ruggedness, practicality, and durability; IPS-N prides itself on being the working man's mech manufacturer, favoring simple solutions like dense armor, heavy tools, and kinetic weapons over flashier, more unpredictable, or overengineered designs. Pilots who want a no-frills workhorse machine that can hold ground or lead the charge, and still keep going when conditions get rough, will find what they're looking for with IPS-N.

SSC

Smith-Shimano Corpro focuses on luxury technology, advanced bio-mechanical integration, and high performance. Its designs are often extremely limited, with licenses only given to those few pilots who are able to afford it, be it through wealth, status. or influence. These frames emphasize speed, precision, stealth, and elegance rather than brute force or overwhelming firepower. SSC equipment is a natural fit for pilots who want the "premium" experience; to move quickly, strike from unexpected angles, or utilize specialized, cutting-edge technology to outmaneuver an opponent.

HORUS

HORUS is a loose network of hackers, radicals, and innovators rather than a conventional corporation. They create strange, eldritch contraptions that favor experimentation, unconventional solutions, and technology that can seem more magic than science. HORUS frames are for the ones who aren't afraid of the untested, paracausal, and outright incomprehensible. Pilots who treat the battlefield like a puzzle; those who enjoy unpredictability, leveraging unusual and esoteric systems to craft problems to force their opponents to solve. Assuming said pilots can get ahold of their schematics in the first place, that is.

HA

Harrison Armory is a powerful military manufacturer that places an emphasis on industrialism, efficiency, and uncompromising design. Its mechs are purpose‐built for firepower, advanced weaponry and defensive systems, reactor management; they are engineering first, all else second. HA is the choice for pilots who want a direct, tactical, heavily-armed approach to enemy suppression, supported by powerful but demanding technology. Its equipment rewards pilots who plan carefully, manage their resources, and are willing to bring overwhelming force to a fight.

Characters that fit

  • A pilot from a settled world who wants to see more of the galaxy.
  • A colony defender protecting a home that larger powers overlook.
  • A mechanic, soldier, courier, researcher, activist, or former corporate employee.
  • A person shaped by cybernetics, artificial intelligence, cloning, or other advanced technology.
  • Someone who believes in Union, distrusts it, or wants a different future entirely.

Ideas that need adjustment

A character does not need to be a professional soldier, but the game is not built around medieval fantasy classes, supernatural magic unrelated to technology, or a character with no connection to the social and political future around them.

A wizard, knight, or superhero can still inspire a concept. Translate the idea into the setting: perhaps the “wizard” is a systems expert, the “knight” is a duelist with a famous mech, and the “superpower” comes from training, equipment, or technology.

The important question is not “Is this character normal?” It is “How does this character belong in a future where people, machines, and institutions are constantly changing?”
