During combat

  1. Ask what the objective is before deciding where to move.
  2. Look at allies and enemies together; LANCER rewards coordinated turns.
  3. Use the action options on your sheet as a menu, not as a test you must already know.
  4. Say your plan out loud. Another player may see a better combination.

Do not worry about finding the perfect move every turn. A useful action might deal damage, create an opening, protect an ally, contest an objective, or force an enemy to spend resources. The important part is helping the team move toward the mission's goal.

Read the mission

Combat encounters are usually about more than destroying every enemy. Pay attention to objectives, zones, extraction points, reinforcements, timers, and characters who need protection. A mech that survives while completing the objective may be more successful than one that deals the most damage.

At the start of a scene, identify what happens if your team ignores the objective. That consequence often tells you where your attention should go first.

Work with the squad

LANCER is designed around characters helping one another. Tell the group what you are trying to accomplish, ask what your allies need, and look for ways to combine your abilities. Supporting another pilot can be just as important as making the final attack.

It is also fine to change your plan. If an enemy moves unexpectedly or an ally needs help, adapt instead of trying to force the original idea.

When you are unsure

Pause and ask the GM. Describe what you want your pilot or mech to attempt, then work backward to the relevant action. The rules are there to help the group decide what happens next.

After the session, COMP/CON can help you review the compendium, maintain your pilot, and prepare for the next mission.

After the mission

Take a few minutes to note what changed. Record new information, unresolved problems, damaged relationships, equipment you want to try, or questions that came up during play. These details can give your pilot direction between missions.

It is also worth checking in with the group about what everyone enjoyed and what they want more of next time. A short conversation can help the campaign stay focused on the kinds of stories and challenges the table finds rewarding.
