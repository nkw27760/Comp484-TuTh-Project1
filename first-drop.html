What kind of game is LANCER?

LANCER usually alternates between narrative play and tactical mech combat. Your pilot explores a science-fiction setting, talks with other characters, and makes decisions. When a conflict becomes a mission, the game focuses on movement, positioning, actions, and teamwork on a map.

That split is helpful: your character can be a complicated person in the story without needing an elaborate combat build on day one. The narrative side may determine why a mission matters, while the combat side asks how your team will accomplish its objective under pressure.

Not every problem needs to become a fight. Talking to people, investigating a location, repairing equipment, or making a difficult decision can be just as important to the campaign as winning a battle.

Ask your group these questions

  • What part of the LANCER setting are we starting in?
  • How much time will we spend in narrative scenes versus combat?
  • Which options from the core rules are available for character creation?
  • Will someone explain the map, ranges, and conditions during the first fight?
  • What tone should I expect: military drama, exploration, political conflict, mystery, or something else?
  • Are there subjects or themes the group wants to avoid during play?

What to prepare

You do not need to read the entire rulebook before joining a game. Bring the character information your group asks for, a way to take notes, and a willingness to ask questions. If the group uses COMP/CON, make sure you know how to open your pilot and mech sheets before the session begins.

It can also help to write down a few details about your pilot: one person they care about, one place they have lived, one reason they accepted the current job, and one thing they do not want to lose.

Build a character concept

Begin with a person rather than a collection of statistics. Decide what your pilot wants, what they are afraid of, and what kind of problem they are willing to risk themselves to solve. The answers do not need to be dramatic; a desire to protect a home, repay a debt, or find a new purpose is enough to begin.

Then connect that concept to the group. A shared employer, hometown, military unit, family connection, or previous mission gives the characters a reason to work together from the first scene.

Know what the table needs

Character creation is a group activity. Share the general direction of your idea before finalizing it, especially if the campaign has a specific location, tone, or starting situation. This gives the GM useful material and helps the other players find connections to your pilot.

You do not need to reveal every detail of your character in advance. Leave room for discoveries, questions, and changes that can emerge naturally during play.

Your first-session checklist

  1. Write one sentence about who your pilot is.
  2. Pick one reason your pilot keeps taking dangerous jobs.
  3. Choose a broad combat feeling: tough, fast, precise, supportive, or strange.
  4. Bring the character sheet or COMP/CON page your group is using.
  5. Write down one question to ask when a rule or choice is unclear.
